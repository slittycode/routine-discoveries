# General discoveries — 2026-08-13

First sweep of the **general** stream (see `routines/general.md`): a broad "what's worth your
time on GitHub" pass, not scoped to audio/MIR or legal-tech (those are covered by the other two
streams — see their own `_seen` lists). Bias is proven usefulness over novelty: real adoption,
recent commits, more than one active contributor (or a maintainer who clearly hasn't walked away).
Format per entry: what it is, evidence of use/maintenance, why it's worth attention, first move.

4 survivors this sweep — fewer than the cap, on purpose: several other candidates (Mythic, a
"RedCode" opencode fork, individual opencode session-history plugins) were checked and dropped for
thin/stale evidence rather than padded in. See "Dropped" at the end.

## [afadil/wealthfolio](https://github.com/afadil/wealthfolio)

**What it is:** A private, local-first investment tracker — Rust/Tauri backend with a
React/TypeScript frontend, SQLite via Diesel, that pulls in your holdings and shows real
performance (twr/mwr, allocation, fees) without shipping your portfolio to anyone's cloud.

**Evidence:** 8.6k stars, 3,485 commits, 319 open issues / 54 open PRs. Commits landed same-day as
this sweep (Aug 9–13 2026) covering a SnapTrade brokerage-sync feature, currency-gain fixes, and a
Japanese locale addition — multiple contributors, not a solo drip-feed. AGPL-3.0.

**Why it's worth your attention:** You said you hold shares you don't understand as well as you
should — this is a tool that makes you actually look at them, and it's built in exactly your hobby
stack (Rust + TypeScript, Tauri). It's a legitimate "read the source to learn Tauri properly"
project as much as a thing to run.

**First move:** `git clone` and run it locally against a read-only export of your holdings before
connecting anything live — see what its performance/allocation views tell you that you didn't
already know. If you'd rather have a self-hosted web dashboard instead of a local desktop app,
[ghostfolio/ghostfolio](https://github.com/ghostfolio/ghostfolio) (9.1k stars, TS/Angular/NestJS,
commits same day) is the equivalent in server form.

## [karakeep-app/karakeep](https://github.com/karakeep-app/karakeep)

**What it is:** A self-hosted "bookmark everything" app — links, notes, screenshots — with
AI-based auto-tagging, full-text + semantic search, and full-page archiving so saved pages don't
rot when the source does.

**Evidence:** 28.3k stars, 2,171 commits, 597 open issues and 80 open PRs — a real, busy community,
not a spike. Weekly-cadence releases through July 2026. Formerly "Hoarder," renamed and still
shipping. AGPL-3.0, self-hostable via Docker, supports local models via Ollama so tagging doesn't
require sending your reading list to OpenAI.

**Why it's worth your attention:** You read a lot and keep more notes than you use — that's
usually a retrieval problem, not a note-taking one. Karakeep's pitch is specifically "save now,
find it again later without re-organizing," which is a lower-friction fit than another PKM tool.

**First move:** Stand it up in Docker with local search only (skip the AI tagging at first), point
your browser extension at it for a week, and see whether the full-text search alone already beats
your current save-and-forget habit.

## [newsboat/newsboat](https://github.com/newsboat/newsboat)

**What it is:** The venerable terminal RSS/Atom reader (successor to Newsbeuter) — vim-style
keybindings, macros, podcast queueing, scriptable filters, nothing but a terminal and a feed list.

**Evidence:** 3.9k stars, 8,951 commits, 389 open issues, 16 open PRs, CI green on GitHub Actions +
Cirrus CI. Over a decade of continuous maintenance — the definition of "old and quietly maintained
beats new and exciting."

**Why it's worth your attention:** You have a half-finished terminal news reader of your own.
Newsboat is worth having installed regardless (it's just a good reader), but it's also the
reference implementation for the hard parts you'll hit — feed parsing edge cases, macro/filter
syntax, offline caching. If you want a closer architectural cousin in a modern stack instead of
C++,
[guyfedwards/nom](https://github.com/guyfedwards/nom) (Go + Bubbletea, 738 stars, v3.0 released
this year, 11 distinct authors across its last 10 commits) is a much smaller codebase that's easier
to read end-to-end in an afternoon and steal ideas from for a Bun/TS build.

**First move:** Install newsboat, live with it for a week to find what you actually miss from it
(that's your spec), then skim nom's source for how it structures fetch/render/cache before writing
more of your own.

## [ratatui/ratatui](https://github.com/ratatui/ratatui)

**What it is:** The standard Rust crate for building terminal UIs — layout, widgets, styling,
input handling — the thing most serious Rust TUI apps (yazi, gitui, bottom, and others) are built
on top of.

**Evidence:** 22.2k stars, 2,307 commits, 148 open issues / 77 open PRs, organized as a workspace
of maintained sub-crates (ratatui-core, ratatui-crossterm, ratatui-widgets). It's itself a survival
story worth knowing: forked from the abandoned `tui-rs` and kept alive by a team rather than one
person — exactly the "one contributor who clearly hasn't abandoned it," except here it's several.

**Why it's worth your attention:** If any part of you wants to build the terminal news reader (or
anything else you're gaming/CrossOver/terminal-adjacent about) in Rust rather than TS, this is the
foundation everyone else already standardized on — you'd be building on well-trodden ground instead
of reinventing input handling and layout.

**First move:** Work through the official tutorial app (a small counter or todo TUI) once, then
decide whether porting your terminal news reader's rendering layer to it is worth doing versus
staying in TS/Bun.

## Dropped (checked, not kept)

- **MythicApp/Mythic** (macOS GPTK-based game launcher, CrossOver-adjacent) — real project, but the
  most recent commit found was mid-February 2026, six months stale as of this sweep, and one
  source reported the sole maintainer had paused development. Worth rechecking in a future sweep;
  not solid enough today.
- **"RedCode" opencode fork** — description (dual TUI/GUI, MCP ecosystem, memory system) reads well
  in write-ups but no verifiable repository with real stars/contributor/commit evidence turned up.
  Skipped rather than reported on marketing copy alone.
- **opencode-history / opencode-sessions-explorer** (session-history plugins for opencode) —
  functionally interesting, but each is a single-author repo with no visible independent adoption.
  Didn't clear the "more than one contributor, or one who clearly hasn't abandoned it" bar; the
  underlying idea (query your own agent session history) may be worth building yourself rather than
  adopting either.
